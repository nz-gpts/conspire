# Research model

For each term, Conspire keeps five things distinct:

- **form** — spelling/lexeme at a particular language stage
- **relationship** — inherited-from, borrowed-from, derived-from, etc.
- **sense** — a meaning supported by evidence
- **attestation** — a dated source demonstrating a form/sense
- **occurrence series** — counts or normalized frequencies from a defined corpus

## Core questions

For an arbitrary word or phrase:

1. What are its documented lexical ancestors?
2. What is the earliest defensible attestation of each form?
3. What meaning is actually demonstrated by each primary source?
4. When do new senses appear?
5. When does public usage depart materially from its previous baseline?
6. Which terms co-occur with it before and after a change point?
7. Are apparent changes corpus effects, historical events, editorial changes, or unresolved?
8. For security terminology, what older/newer formulations describe related concepts?

## Guardrails

- Never infer intent from word frequency alone.
- Never treat unusual vocabulary as proof of malicious activity.
- Preserve source, date, corpus, retrieval method and uncertainty.
- Separate observed change points from explanations for those change points.
- LLM classifications are annotations, not primary evidence.
