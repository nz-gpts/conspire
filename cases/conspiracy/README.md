# Reference case: conspiracy

This case will compare lexical ancestry with the historical public record.

## Starting hypotheses — not findings

- The lexical ancestry and the history of actual English meanings may not align neatly.
- Different corpora may show different periods of increased visibility.
- **conspiracy**, **conspiracy theory**, **conspiracy theorist**, and related forms must be measured separately.
- A rise in occurrence does not establish why the rise happened.

## First extraction targets

- Etymology Tree relationship data
- etymology-db relationship data
- Etymonline-derived dated prose
- primary historical attestations
- long-run print frequency
- web-era dated occurrence/frequency sources

All findings should be reproducible from stored source metadata or retrieval instructions.
